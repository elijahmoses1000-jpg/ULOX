# Tests

## Test 1 - A new arrival is told exactly what to do, and does it [sanity]
Viewport: desktop
Covers: first-run onboarding, the six-discipline journey, next-step guidance, curriculum reading, progress persistence
Description: Verifies a confused first-time visitor is given one next action and that completing it sticks.

Steps:
1. Read the welcome screen and click "Makes sense"
2. Choose "Beginner" and click "Next"
3. Click "Start the journey"
4. Click the "Read this" button on the next-step card
5. Click "Got it" in the dialog that opens
6. Refresh the page

Expected: The journey home shows a next step naming a step number out of 6, and after the refresh step 1 "Ask properly" shows at least 1 of its readings complete.

## Test 2 - Switching skill level rewrites the interface
Viewport: desktop
Covers: level switcher control, glossary-driven wording, persistence of the chosen level
Description: Verifies the level toggle visibly changes vocabulary rather than only storing a preference.

Steps:
1. Click "Skip" on the welcome screen, then open "Practice" from the navigation and note the page heading
2. Open the skill level control in the header and choose "Advanced"
3. Return to "Practice"

Expected: The Practice page heading reads "Puzzles" while Beginner is selected and reads "Tactics" once Advanced is selected.

## Test 3 - Two players race a rated match and chat
Viewport: desktop
Covers: match creation, join-by-code, answer submission and score sync, match chat across actors, realtime room updates
Description: Verifies the multiplayer room connects two separate clients and keeps their boards in step.

Steps:
1. As Actor A, click "Skip" on the welcome screen, open "Match a friend" and click "Create match", then note the six-character match code
2. As Actor B, click "Skip" on the welcome screen, open "Match a friend", enter that match code and click "Join"
3. As Actor B, choose any one of the four candidate moves on the first position
4. As Actor A, type "Good luck" into the match chat and send it

Expected: Actor A's screen shows Actor B as the opponent instead of "Waiting…", Actor B's progress reads 1 of 5, and the message "Good luck" is visible in Actor B's match chat.

## Test 6 - See the shape, publish a build, then remove it again
Viewport: desktop
Covers: build blueprint mind map, publish a build, published-build listing, author-only removal, destructive-action confirmation
Description: Verifies the Build screen explains the target architecture before wiring, and that a build you publish is yours to unpublish.

Steps:
1. Click "Skip" on the welcome screen, then open "Build" from the navigation
2. Read the "Plan" panel, which is open by default and lists the parts to add in order
3. Click "See the shape" and read the diagram, then click "Start building"
4. Click "Share build", type "Research loop that stops when it has enough" as the title and click "Publish"
5. Open "Published" from the navigation and select the "Mine" tab
6. Click "Remove" on the build titled "Research loop that stops when it has enough", then click "Yes, remove"
7. Refresh the page and select the "Mine" tab again

Expected: The Plan panel and the diagram both name the parts and the arrow conditions before the build starts; after publishing, the build appears under "Mine"; after removal and the refresh it is gone from the list.

## Test 7 - Add an output and give it the contract that stops the loop
Viewport: desktop
Covers: node catalog search, 30-part palette, adding a typed node, outcome contract editor on an output node
Description: Verifies the parts bin offers far more than the seven primitives and that an output node asks for the Outcome contract that tells a loop when to stop.

Steps:
1. Click "Skip" on the welcome screen, then open "Build" from the navigation
2. Open the "Edit" tab and read the part count shown beside "Add a node"
3. Type "escalation" into the part search field
4. Click the "Escalation" result to add it to the canvas
5. Click the new Escalation node on the canvas
6. Open the "Outcome" field and type "Hand to a human with the request, what was tried, and why it stopped. The run ends here."

Expected: The palette reports 30 parts across named groups; after the search only Escalation-related parts remain; the selected Escalation node shows an "Outcome contract" section, and the Outcome field is marked filled once the text is entered.

## Test 8 - Play a game against UX and get a reason for every move
Viewport: mobile
Covers: Play hub, the UX opponent, engine-scored move grading, per-move explanations, match settlement
Description: Verifies the bot opponent plays real engine moves on its own board and explains each one, and that the match settles on the two scores.

Steps:
1. Click "Skip" on the welcome screen, then open "Play" from the navigation
2. Click "Play UX"
3. Choose the "Learner" level
4. Pick one of the offered moves, six times in a row

Expected: Both scores start equal and change as moves are played; the move log shows a grade and a sentence for each of your moves and a move with a reason for each of UX's; after the sixth move the page declares a winner or a draw based on the two scores.
